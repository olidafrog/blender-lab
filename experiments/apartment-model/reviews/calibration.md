# Calibration review: A and B

## 1. Score

- **A: 5.9 / 10.** Brick colour 5.5, brick pattern 5.5, floor colour 6.0, floor pattern 6.4.
- **B: 5.7 / 10.** Brick colour 5.5, brick pattern 5.5, floor colour 5.8, floor pattern 6.0.

## 2. Targets

- Brick/paint: 0.22 in photo 2 (target 0.21) and 0.26 in photo 5 (target 0.25). Hit.
- Floor/paint, photo 2: A 0.93 (just missed), B 0.87 (hit). Target 0.89.
- Floor/paint, photo 5: 0.95 in both. Target 0.99: just missed.
- Courses (about 85 mm), planks (700 × 140 mm) and the spine along the room: hit by eye.

## 3. What works

- The herringbone layout, plank proportions and thin dark seams match rows 5 and 8.
- The flame figure is believable in A's row 2 floor.

## 4. Problems, ranked

1. **The brick is too uniform, and the mortar reads as a pale grid** (row 1 pier, row 5 wall). The bricks are one chocolate tone, and the crisp pale joints make the wall look like brick slips. The photo shows a large brick-to-brick spread, dusty patches and dark, near-flush joints. **Fix:** set the mortar to about the brick value plus 10 %. Make the joints shallow and soft. Add a ±25 % per-brick value spread and a large dust mask.
2. **The lit reveals are mauve, not orange-red** (row 1 left detail, row 5 arch). **Fix:** shift the hue about 10° toward red and add saturation.
3. **The floor is too blond, clean and even** (row 2 floor, both row 8 crops). Row 2's detail is about 30 % lighter than the photo. The cracks, knots and grey cast are missing. B is worse: its planks are more uniform and its sheen reads as lacquer. **Fix:** add dark cracks and knots, raise the contrast within each plank, pull toward grey-brown and set roughness to about 0.4.

## 5. Research check

Bond, courses, planks and spine agree. "Mortar slightly paler than the brick" over-reads: in the photos the joints barely show. The photo floor has a rustic figure with cracks and knots, not clean flat-sawn oak.

## 6. What 8.5 needs

- Dark, near-flush mortar.
- A strong per-brick spread with dust patches.
- A redder brick, so the lit reveals read orange.
- Floor cracks and knots, with more contrast within each plank.
- A floor 15 to 30 % darker and greyer in photo 2's detail.

## Comparison

A is closer. Its floor keeps more plank-to-plank variation and grain contrast, while B evens the planks out and adds a gloss the photos do not show.
