## 3. What works

## 4. Problems, ranked

1. **The fabric is too smooth and too grey (all rows, 1:1).** Row 2, near arm: the photo shows a streaky slub with flecks; the render has about a third of that contrast and reads as felt. The photo's fabric is about 8 % warmer than its wall; the render's is 3 % cooler. Fix: bring back P's slub contrast at the current scale, and warm the albedo to the swatch's B/R (0.90) against the wall.
2. **The blocks are slabs, not cushions (rows 1 and 3).** Row 1: the photo's seat fronts bulge and the gaps open into dark Vs; the render's gaps are thin and parallel. Row 3: the back block is flat with a crisp top edge; the photo shows a rolled top with a highlight. Fix: 2–3 cm face bulge, 4–6 cm edge roll, tops domed about 2 cm.
3. **The window-lit tops are too dark (row 1 ottoman top, row 3 arm inner face).** Fix: let the domed ottoman top catch the window, add a soft grazing sheen, and hit the photo 3 arm target.

## 5. Research check

Construction, sizes, feet and flanges agree. Three claims fail: the flange is straight and even, with no "wavy lip" or "pinched ears"; the faces are not "softly puffed"; the fabric does not read as a "slubby plain weave" at 1:1.

## 6. What 8.5 needs

- Photo-level slub contrast, and fabric warmer than the wall.
- Puffed faces, rolled edges and V-gaps.
- A wavy flange with corner ears, and sitting wrinkles on the seats.
- The ottoman top and arm face on target.
