4. **Problems, ranked**
1. **Left side and pods: third round with no change (x 130–660, y 90–760).** The dial still lies face-up on a bright chrome triangle. The gear lies flat. The pods are fat cans with teal caps on a floating block. The reference has a gunmetal side frame, a gear standing on the left wall, and long gunmetal barrels built into the top shell. Value tweaks will not fix this, so rebuild these parts from `ref_front.jpg`. Model the side frame as a curved plate with a 3 mm fillet. Stand the gear at 60–80° to the top. Make the pods 2.5× longer than wide, with knurled rings. Steel base 0.35–0.5, roughness 0.3–0.4.
2. **Lower block reads as a flat tray (x 820–1560, y 760–1100).** The battery is a recessed plate with a chrome rim and a drawn diagonal line. The chrome cylinder at the lower right has no match in the reference. The reference battery is a separate raised block with a sloped top, an INSERT latch and a gunmetal bracket on its left. It is cut off from the shell by a black gap about 2 mm wide. Rebuild it as a solid block. Remove the chrome rim and the cylinder, and put the rubber cord plug at the lower right. The gaps must go black: p5 ≤ 12 and <12 % ≥ 5.3. Thin creases do not move p5, so the gaps must be 1.5–2.5 mm wide and deep, with a shell overhang.
3. **Polymer finish is smooth (all tops).** There is no bead-blast speckle. The wear on the lower-right walls is a chalky band 15–25 px wide that looks like stucco. The scratches on the DT plate are still curly hairs. Add a fine bump noise (scale 0.2–0.4 mm, strength 0.05–0.1). Limit wear to the fillet crest with a curvature mask, 3–6 px wide. Use straight, sparse scratch strokes. Raise the top albedo so the tops read 70–85, and bring the slope back to 90–105.
