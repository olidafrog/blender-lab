## Research findings

- The photo is a shift-lens architectural photograph taken from a raised position across the street; verticals are parallel by design.
- The building is a real RC frame with brick infill. The coloured relief is cast plaster in hand-carved moulds (0.1–0.3 m proud), painted with synthetic paint; the thin orange and yellow lines are hand-painted. The glass is dark reflective curtain wall in thin gold-coloured frames.
- The sun comes from the front-right (about 45° off the facade normal, about 35° high): shadows fall to the left of proud elements.
- The photo's sky is brighter and paler than a physical sky (the photographer's exposure); the render lifts the sky for the camera only, by design.
- Scale is about 38 px per metre at the facade: the building is about 29 m wide and 20 m tall.

## What to judge

1. **Photorealism.** Could this pass as a photograph of a real building? Name every CG tell: too-perfect edges or planes, uniform materials, missing dirt or wear, lighting that is flat or wrong, reflections that look fake, missing context.
2. **Modelling fidelity.** The facade's layout, the depth and layering of the mouldings, the stepped motifs, the oculi and diamonds, the corner towers, the roof tanks, the left corner, the ground floor.
3. **Materials.** Paint, reflective glass and frames, shutters, pavement, street, brick.
4. **Light.** Sun direction and hardness, shadow lengths and colour, sky, exposure, bounce.

## Do not penalise

- The framing and the camera: they are fixed to the reference.
- The people, cars, cables and signs of daily life that the photo happened to catch, unless their absence makes the scene read as a model.
- Slight softness of sub-pixel lines (mullions, shutter slats) at this size: the final is rendered at 2x and downsampled.

## Calibration

- 5 = generic; a stock render with the right subject.
- 7 = good; clearly the right idea, visible problems side by side.
- 8.5 = ship it; only minor differences side by side with the references.
Use one decimal place.

## How to look

Open the full frame first, then every 1:1 crop. Crops show aliasing, seams, noise and texture the full frame hides.

## Output format

Write exactly these sections to the review file you are given, in under 450 words:

1. **Score:** N.N / 10
2. **Targets** — each numeric target: measured value, hit or missed.
3. **What works** — up to 3 bullets.
4. **Problems, ranked** — at most 3, most damaging first. For each: where in the frame, what is wrong, and a concrete CG fix. If a value tweak has clearly not fixed it, name a different mechanism. If you ask for "more" or "less" of something, give the acceptable range.
5. **Research check** — does the image agree with the research findings above? Name any claim the image contradicts.
6. **What 8.5 needs** — the shortest list of changes that would get there.
