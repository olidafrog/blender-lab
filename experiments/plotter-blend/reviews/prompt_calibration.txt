## Research findings

- The render is a raster of two SVG files made of strokes only (no fills), drawn side by side on paper grey: the sphere on the left, the funnel on the right. Each is a pen-plotter drawing on an A5 page. Judge it as a plot.
- The sphere is a contour drawing: level sets of "latitude + one raised bump + one sunken bump" on a sphere. That gives parallel bands, closed rings round two eyes, and an S-shaped band between them.
- The funnel is a wireframe of a catenoid (surface of revolution): meridians × parallels, both sides drawn.
- A plotter cannot fill, so each dot is one small spiral stroke.

## Failure checklist

1. Sphere: does each eye have at least two closed rings with no free ends?
2. Sphere: is there one clear S-shaped band through the middle, between the eyes?
3. Sphere: does the outline of the line set read as a round ball (no notches, flats or spikes at the edge)?
4. Sphere: does every line end on a dot, with no bare ends?
5. Sphere: are there places where lines bunch or overlap into a dark mass a pen would blot?
6. Funnel: does the profile match the reference (wide thin lip, sides near vertical at the throat)?
7. Funnel: is there a dot at every crossing, and none floating off a line?
8. Both: are all lines smooth (no kinks, facets or stray fragments)?

Answer each with yes or no and the evidence.

## What to judge

Form fidelity to the two reference icons; line and dot character; how well it would plot (no doubled lines, no blots, no fragments); overall craft as a monoline technical illustration.

## Do not penalise

Page layout, paper tone, the relative size of the two forms.

## Sub-scores

Sphere form 35 %, funnel form 25 %, line and dot character 20 %, plot readiness 20 %.

## Calibration

- 5 = generic; a stock render with the right subject.
- 7 = good; clearly the right idea, visible problems side by side.
- 8.5 = ship it; only minor differences side by side with the references.
Use one decimal place.
